Method

One rubric, any volume

Every score is read against the same categories and the same three severities, so five scores in a sprint and 300 in a quarter produce results that can be compared release to release. Each finding is logged as measure, staff, category, severity, what the original shows and what the output shows.

CriticalA musician would play or sing something wrong: pitch, accidental, duration, clef, key, meter. MajorThe notes survive but the meaning doesn’t: a dynamic, articulation, tuplet or lyric syllable. MinorCosmetic, or obvious from context. Counted, not itemized.

Orchestra

These Violent Delights, p. 32

mm. 144–146 · 2017 · read by the Curtis Symphony Orchestra

What a model has to get right

  • Thirty-one staves, with player numbers (1., 2., 3., a2) inside shared staves.
  • Four trumpets on one staff, splitting into two voices and back.
  • Divisi violins on paired staves, then “non div.” in the middle of a bar.
  • Clef changes in the middle of a system.
  • Nine and seven against six and three in the woodwinds.
String quartet

Entanglements, p. 10

mm. 85–94 · 2015 · premiered by the Aizuri Quartet

What a model has to get right

  • Trills with an accidental above the sign, which belongs to the trill and not to the note.
  • Trill lines that cross barlines.
  • Measured tremolo on double stops.
  • Quintuplets and sextuplets whose slurs cross the beam groups.
Voice and piano

Map You, p. 3

mm. 14–22 · art song

What a model has to get right

  • Lyrics with melismas and extender lines, attached to the right note.
  • A new meter almost every bar.
  • Grace notes before chords, and a quintuplet inside a slur.
  • An 8va line over part of the right hand only.
Six voices

INNERVISIONS of a Trebled Soul in a Troubled America, p. 8

mm. 46–50 · 2017 · premiered by Variant Six

What a model has to get right

  • IPA vowel changes in the lyric line, such as [ɑ] → [i].
  • Glissando contours drawn between noteheads.
  • Unmeasured time, with stemless repeated notes.
  • Spoken text under a sustained x-notehead.
Amplified tuba and percussion

Trust Fall, II. Rupture

mm. 31–55 · 2019 · commissioned and premiered by BrassTaps Duo

What a model has to get right

  • Unpitched percussion with a different notehead for each instrument.
  • A drawn pitch contour in place of notes, for four bars.
  • Effects-pedal cues in boxes, which are text and not notes.
  • A long 8vb line that changes every pitch underneath it.
Tenor and mixed ensemble

What I Did For Love, p. 22

Rehearsal 80a · 2018 · commissioned and premiered by the CHAI Collaborative Ensemble · text by Darrel Holnes

What a model has to get right

  • No meter: timings in seconds, with boxed material played freely.
  • Feathered beams, which mean an accelerando and not a rhythm.
  • x-noteheads mixed with pitched notes on one staff.
  • “Repeat until 80b” in place of written bars.
Tenor and mixed ensemble

What I Did For Love, p. 24

Rehearsal 80c · 2018

What a model has to get right

  • Two tempos at once: a slowing trio, and strings looping a boxed 5/4 pattern.
  • Numbered pulses under the piano, which are counts and not fingering.
  • Staging directions kept apart from technique text.
  • The tenor’s lyrics and breath marks inside a gradual tempo change.
Amplified tuba and percussion

Trust Fall, V. Grace

mm. 174–188 · 2019

What a model has to get right

  • A mobile form: three looping percussion cells, each joined to its entry point in the tuba part.
  • Bars measured in seconds instead of beats.
  • Two tempo marks working at once.
  • Performance text (HOLD, DELAY) that must be kept and must not be read as notes.
